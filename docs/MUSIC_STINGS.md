# Music & Sting Direction (Draft v1)

> The sound doctrine: acoustic, warm, and region-native. Nothing synthetic where a
> real-world texture could carry it. All TBD by BudE.

## The Score

- **The Hidden Hollow (home)** — the main theme: fingerpicked strings around a
  campfire pulse. Every region's theme is a variation on THIS melody, grown from the
  same seed — the whole soundtrack is one family (the game's thesis, in music).
- **Region 1 Old Growth** — the theme at its warmest: acoustic guitar, moss-soft
  strings, forest floor percussion (wood taps, rustle swishes).
- **Region 2 Frostcap Peaks** — airy bells and breath-tones over wind; melody slowed
  and spacious like altitude.
- **Region 3 Red Dust** — handclaps, foot-stomps, and fast-strummed runs; playful and
  show-offy — Sunsprint's region.
- **Region 4 Still Water** — low croon, bowed strings, frog-tick rhythm; quietest track.
- **Region 5 Deep Green** — the full theme in the fullest arrangement: every region's
  instrument layered — the whole world playing at once, because the finale.

## Boss Music Law

Each villain gets the region theme "possessed": same melody, wrong intentions —
colder, faster, heavier. The Wendigo's theme is the home theme played like a heartbeat
in deep woods. When the final hit lands, the boss theme breaks back into the WARM
version mid-phrase — the victory IS the music resolving.

## Stings (the fixed family)

- **Totem relight** — the gold chime family (established): the checkpoint sound's
  resolving chord. Same sting in all five regions, so the player learns it means
  "safe again."
- **Big Token pickup** — short bright pluck, pitched into the region's key.
- **Checkpoint totem** — low warm bell (a promise, not a celebration).
- **Boss intro** — each villain's sting is their theme's first three notes, played
  slow and clear. Kid learns the sting, kid learns the fear.
- **Boss defeat** — the region theme resolving, gold chime layered on top.
- **Villain defeated stomp-off** — the comedy beat: a little brass raspberry. Every
  villain gets it. It keeps the Bowser law funny, not scary.

## Ambient Law

Wind, water, and creature beds are diegetic (they live in the world); music is not.
Bosses' "voice" moments (Mossback's snore bubbles, Vineheart's "STAY.") are world
sounds, not dialogue-heavy VO — the game stays light on text.

*(Studio splash stings are separate and locked: BudE Vision uses the isolated pig
snort, attack-only, per the standing studio rule.)*
